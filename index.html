How a part is made decides how it performs.

I'm a master's student in Mechanical Engineering at Clemson University, advised by Dr. Rodrigo Martinez-Duarte in the Multiscale Manufacturing Laboratory. I work where manufacturing processes meet material behavior: additive manufacturing, stimuli-responsive metamaterials, and the testing that shows whether a process did its job. Before Clemson, I spent two years as a mechanical engineer designing engine components and characterizing alloy steels.

A

From process to performance

Materials engineers think in a chain: processing sets structure, structure sets properties, and properties set performance. My interests follow that chain, and so does most of the work I've done so far.

  1. Process

    01

    How is it made?

    • Additive manufacturing
    • Process optimization
    • Intelligent process control
    • Built an FDM 3D printer for my senior thesis
    • Prototyped JF-17 ventral fins by directed energy deposition

  2. Structure

    02

    What forms inside it?

    • Microstructure
    • Metamaterial architecture
    • Layer adhesion and porosity in FDM-printed parts
    • Metallography of H13 and 42CrMo4 steels

  3. Property

    03

    How does it respond to load and heat?

    • Material characterization
    • Tensile, bend, torsion, compression and hardness testing
    • OES chemical analysis and SEM
    • Thermal simulation of steel and aluminum in ANSYS

  4. Performance

    04

    What can it do in service?

    • Stimuli-responsive 4D printing
    • Component design
    • Review of 4D-printed metamaterials, in preparation
    • Engine parts: rocker covers, cylinder heads, pistons
    • Stress and fracture at gear-tooth roots and shaft necks
